Methodology

The Somalia Municipality Risk Dashboard integrates multiple geospatial datasets to assess hazard exposure, vulnerability, and overall disaster risk across municipalities. Each hazard layer is modelled independently before being combined with exposure and vulnerability indicators to produce standardized municipality-level risk information. The full technical methodology, including data sources, modelling approaches, and validation procedures, is documented in the documentation section of the dashboard.

How the Risk Dashboard is Built

Each hazard layer is independently modelled and normalised to a common 0–10 risk scale. Hazard, exposure, and vulnerability indicators are integrated at the municipality level to support evidence-based planning, investment prioritisation, and disaster risk reduction.

1. Hazard Modelling

Hazard layers quantify the likelihood and intensity of natural and human-induced hazards affecting municipalities.

  • Flood extents derived from hydrodynamic simulations for 5-, 10-, 50-, 100-, and 250-year return periods.
  • Drought severity estimated using SPI, SPEI, and NDVI anomalies.
  • Conflict hazard generated from ACLED event density and spatial clustering.

2. Exposure Assessment

Exposure datasets identify the population, infrastructure, and assets located within hazard-prone areas.

  • WorldPop population distribution.
  • Global Human Settlement Layer (GHSL) built-up surface.
  • OpenStreetMap road network.
  • OCHA IDP site catalogue.
  • National schools and health facility registers.

3. Vulnerability Assessment

Socio-economic indicators are used to estimate how susceptible communities are to disaster impacts.

  • Poverty headcount.
  • Female-headed households.
  • Population under five years of age.
  • Access to improved water sources.

4. Risk Aggregation

All indicators are standardised and weighted using expert judgement. The resulting municipality risk index is validated against historical disaster impacts and available ground-reference information before publication.

Hazard Layers

Flood

Current Level: High

Riverine and pluvial flooding are modelled using hydrodynamic simulations for multiple return periods.

Drought

Current Level: Critical

Drought severity is classified using SPI, SPEI, and NDVI anomalies against long-term climatic baselines.

Conflict

Current Level: Elevated

Conflict exposure is derived from ACLED conflict event density aggregated to municipality boundaries.

Data Sources

The dashboard integrates authoritative national and international geospatial datasets, including:

  • Copernicus Emergency Management Service (EMS)
  • Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 satellite imagery
  • WorldPop 2020 Population Dataset (100 m)
  • Global Human Settlement Layer (GHSL)
  • OpenStreetMap (OSM)
  • Armed Conflict Location & Event Data Project (ACLED)
  • OCHA IDP Site Directory
  • FAO SWALIM Hydrological Data
  • Somalia National Bureau of Statistics (SNBS)
